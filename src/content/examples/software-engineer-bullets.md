## Before

> 负责后端开发，参与系统优化，对接多个部门。

This bullet is vague: it doesn't say what was built, what improved, or what the scope was. Every word is a generalisation.

## After

> Spearheaded the migration of the payments service to a Kafka-based event architecture, cutting mean processing latency from 320 ms to 90 ms.

Three changes made the difference:

1. **A precise verb** — "Spearheaded" says the work was initiated and driven by you, rather than "responsible for", which only states accountability.
2. **A concrete object** — the payments service and Kafka are named, so the skill is unambiguous.
3. **A quantified outcome** — the latency change (320 ms → 90 ms) gives the claim weight.

## Why "Spearheaded" and not "Led"

In this fictional example, the engineer initiated the migration rather than inheriting it. "Led" would understate the initiating role. If the engineer had instead taken over an existing migration, "Led" would be the honest choice — and "Managed" might fit if the main contribution was coordination.

## What this example is not

This is an illustration of a writing pattern, not a template to copy. Your own bullets should reflect what you actually did and what actually changed. If you can't quantify the outcome, say what you did and the scope you worked in — specificity without honesty is worse than a plain bullet.
