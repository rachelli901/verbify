## University name: use the official English name

Chinese universities publish official English names. Use those exactly — don't translate on your own. For example:

- **正确** — Tsinghua University
- **不要写** — Qinghua University
- **正确** — Peking University
- **不要写** — Beijing University

If you're unsure of the official English name, check the university's website or your diploma.

## Degree titles

Common Chinese degree titles and their conventional English rendering:

| Chinese | Conventional English |
|---|---|
| 本科 / 学士学位 | Bachelor's degree (B.Sc. / B.A. / B.Eng.) |
| 硕士 / 硕士学位 | Master's degree (M.Sc. / M.A. / M.Eng.) |
| 博士 | Ph.D. |

Be specific where you can: "B.Eng. in Computer Science" is clearer than just "Bachelor's degree".

## 985 and 211

985 和 211 是中国的高等教育工程，英文语境下通常不被理解。几种处理方式：

- **For international roles** — these labels carry little meaning. Consider omitting, or briefly noting "top-tier university" only if it's well-established (and only for the most selective schools).
- **For roles within China or among Chinese employers** — 985/211 仍然有意义，可以保留。
- **A safer approach** — let the university name speak for itself, and lead with your outcomes (projects, grades, awards) instead.

## Major and minor subjects

List your major in English. If your major doesn't have a clean English equivalent, use the closest recognised term and, if helpful, add a one-line description. Avoid literal translations that obscure meaning.

## GPA and academic achievements

- Include GPA only if it's strong and relevant. State the scale: "GPA 3.8 / 4.0".
- Awards, scholarships and rankings can be powerful — state them plainly with the year.
