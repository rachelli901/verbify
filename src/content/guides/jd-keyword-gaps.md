## The goal: alignment, not repetition

Keyword matching is about showing that your experience genuinely covers what the role needs. It is not about copying the JD into your resume until the words match.

## A simple four-step method

### 1. Pull the real requirements from the JD

Look for nouns that name skills, tools, methods and scope:

> *Required: 5+ years of experience with distributed systems, Kafka, and incident response…*

The meaningful keywords here are: `distributed systems`, `Kafka`, `incident response`.

### 2. Map each requirement to something you've actually done

If you've worked with Kafka, say so — with a result. If you haven't, don't claim it. Instead, find the adjacent skill you do have and explain the transfer.

### 3. Use the JD's language where it fits

If the JD says "cross-functional collaboration", and your experience genuinely matches, use that phrase. Consistency helps, but only when it's truthful.

### 4. Keep outcomes front and centre

A keyword alone is weak. Pair it with scope and result:

> *Led incident response for a distributed payments system (Kafka-based), cutting mean recovery time from 45 to 12 minutes.*

## What "keyword stuffing" looks like

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| Repeat the same JD phrase five times | Reads as forced; recruiters notice |
| Claim a skill you don't have | Risky in interviews and often easy to spot |
| List tools without context | A bare tool name proves nothing |

## A note on ATS systems

There is no universal "ATS pass score", and different employers use different systems. Focus on clarity, relevance and truthfulness. A readable, well-structured resume will serve you across any system — and with any human reviewer.
