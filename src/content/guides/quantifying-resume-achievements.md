## Why numbers matter

An English resume bullet that says what you did is useful. One that says what changed because of it is persuasive. Recruiters skim dozens of resumes in minutes; a number gives them a fast, concrete anchor for your impact.

This is not about impressing anyone with big figures. It is about giving the reader a sense of **scale** — how big was the thing you touched, and what moved after you acted.

## Four buckets of metrics

When you review your bullets, try to attach at least one number from these four categories:

| Bucket | What it answers | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Scale** | How big was the system, team, or user base? | team of 8, 1.2M users, $4M budget |
| **Efficiency** | Did you save time or money? | cut processing time 40%, saved 12 hrs/week |
| **Quality** | Did outcomes get better? | raised pass rate from 82% to 94% |
| **Growth** | Did something expand? | grew signups 3× in two quarters |

## Before → after

| Weak bullet | Quantified bullet |
|---|---|
| Managed the customer support team. | **Led** a support team of 9, cutting average response time from 14h to 6h. |
| Improved the reporting process. | **Streamlined** the weekly report, saving 10 hours across 5 analysts. |
| Helped increase sales. | **Supported** a regional launch that grew Q3 revenue 18% YoY. |
| Worked on the migration project. | **Delivered** a database migration for 1.2M accounts with zero downtime. |

## What if you don't have exact numbers

You rarely need the precise figure. A reasonable estimate, clearly framed, beats a vague sentence.

- If you know the range, use it: *"handled 30–40 support tickets per day"*.
- If you know the direction and order of magnitude, say so: *"reduced onboarding time by roughly half"*.
- If the work was a team effort, state your share: *"owned the API layer of a project that shipped to 200k users"*.

Do not invent precise percentages you cannot defend in an interview. Recruiters probe specifics, and a number you cannot explain hurts more than no number.

## Pair numbers with verbs

Quantification works best when joined to a precise action verb. "Responsible for improving efficiency" tells the reader nothing; "**Reduced** build time 35% by splitting the CI pipeline" tells them exactly what you did and what changed.

Verbify's verb suggestions flag weak or vague verbs so you can replace them before you add the metric.

## Common pitfalls

- **One number per bullet is enough** — stacking three metrics in one line buries the point.
- **Don't attach numbers to fluff** — "attended 12 meetings" is not an achievement.
- **Be consistent with units** — pick hours or days, not both, across similar bullets.
