## The short answer

For **US and UK** applications, leave out: photo, age, date of birth, marital status, gender, ethnicity and ID numbers. These details are generally considered irrelevant to hiring decisions and are often avoided to prevent unconscious bias.

For **Singapore and Malaysia**, conventions are more mixed — some local employers still expect a photo. But for regional or multinational roles, the international convention (no photo) is increasingly common.

## Country-by-country

| Field | US / UK / AU | SG / MY |
|---|---|---|
| Photo | Leave out | Mixed — omit for MNC/regional roles |
| Age / DOB | Leave out | Leave out |
| Marital status | Leave out | Leave out |
| ID / NRIC / passport | Leave out | Leave out |
| Gender | Leave out | Leave out |
| Address | City + country is enough | City is enough |
| Nationality | Only if visa/permit requires it | Same |

## Why this matters

Including unnecessary personal information can trigger unconscious bias — and in some jurisdictions, asking for it can create compliance issues for the employer. Keeping your resume focused on skills and outcomes is both safer and more effective.

## What belongs instead

Replace personal detail with outcomes. A resume should answer:

- What did you do?
- What changed because of it?
- What skills and scope did you demonstrate?

## A note on identity and visa status

Some applications do require nationality, work-authorisation or visa status — typically in a dedicated field on the application form, not on the resume itself. Follow the employer's instructions for those specific fields. Verbify does not provide immigration or visa advice; check with the employer or an authorised channel.
