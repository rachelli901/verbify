## Why tailor at all

Most companies filter resumes with software that scans for the posting's keywords before a human reads them. A resume that mirrors the job description's language is more likely to surface — and, just as importantly, easier for the recruiter to match to the role.

Tailoring does **not** mean inventing experience. It means presenting the experience you already have in the words the employer uses.

## Step 1 — mirror the posting's verbs

Job descriptions repeat certain verbs on purpose: *"collaborate"*, *"optimize"*, *"own"*, *"drive"*. If those verbs describe real parts of your work, use them.

| Job description says | Your bullet could say |
|---|---|
| "Own the release process" | **Owned** the release process across 4 teams |
| "Optimize latency" | **Optimized** API latency from 320ms to 90ms |
| "Collaborate with design" | **Collaborated** with design on 3 launches |

## Step 2 — reorder, don't rewrite

You rarely need to rewrite bullets. Move the most relevant ones to the top of each role, and lead with the verb that matches the posting.

- Before: a generic list with your strongest, most relevant bullet buried at the bottom.
- After: that same bullet moved up and reworded to the posting's verb.

## Step 3 — cut what does not fit

If a bullet has no link to the role and no strong outcome, consider dropping it for this application. A shorter, relevant resume beats a longer, scattered one.

## Before → after (one role)

**Generic:**
> Responsible for the data pipeline and some reporting.

**Tailored to a "Data Engineer — streaming pipelines" posting:**
> **Built** a streaming pipeline ingesting 2M events/day; **optimized** latency 70% via partitioned topics.

The second version uses the posting's verbs (*built*, *optimized*) and leads with relevance.

## What to do next

Keep one master resume, then produce a tailored version per application by reordering and re-verbing the top bullets. The master stays honest; the tailored copy stays relevant.
